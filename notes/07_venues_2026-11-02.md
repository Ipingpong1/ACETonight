## 1. What short formats exist in public health/epidemiology, how they differ from math "letters", and do they fit a DIF paper?

### Takeaway
Public health and epidemiology have three short tiers:
- **Research letters:** 600–800 words, 1–2 display items, ~8–10 references, no abstract. They are peer-reviewed original research, but they leave essentially no room for methods.
- **Research briefs / brief reports / brief communications:** 1,200–2,000 words, a (usually structured) abstract, 3–4 display items, often with an online appendix.
- **Research notes:** in survey methodology, up to about 3,000 words.

The format that resembles math letters in spirit (short, rapid, but substantive) is the **research brief / brief report**, not the research letter. A latent-variable DIF paper whose claim depends on anchor choice and sensitivity analyses fits a brief (with an appendix) or a full article, not a letter.

### Cited Findings
**Research letters (strictest short format)**
- JAMA Network Open Research Letter: "should not exceed 800 words of text and 10 references," "may include up to 2 small tables or figures," and "should not include an abstract or key points." Online supplements are "only allowed for brief additional and absolutely necessary methods," "not for any additional results or discussion." Any study type eligible as an Original Investigation may be submitted as a letter — [JAMA Netw Open Instructions for Authors, fetched](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/pages/instructions-for-authors)
- AJPM Research Letters "provide a brief and timely report of outstanding original research (e.g., the result of a pilot study)," "undergo external peer review," need no abstract, may include one table or figure, and are limited to 700 words and 10 references (author instructions r. 3/3/2023) — [AJPM Author Instructions PDF, fetched](https://legacyfileshare.elsevier.com/promis_misc/AMEPRE_gfa.pdf)
- Epidemiology (LWW) Research Letters: "up to 600 words, 1 table or figure, 8 references" — [Karim, "Choosing epidemiological journals," 25 Mar 2024, secondary](https://ehsanx.github.io/choosing-epidemiological-journals). The official LWW page returned HTTP 402 to the fetcher: [journals.lww.com/epidem](https://journals.lww.com/epidem/pages/informationforauthors.aspx).
- American Journal of Epidemiology (not on the candidate list, but a comparison point): Research Letter of 1,500 words, no abstract, "Only 1 figure and 1 short table are permitted" — [Karim 2024, secondary](https://ehsanx.github.io/choosing-epidemiological-journals)
- PCD's "Letter" type (600 words, ≤6 references, ≤2 display items) is only for responses to a previously published PCD article. It is not a vehicle for new research — [PCD Types of Articles, fetched](https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/for_authors/types_of_articles.htm)

**Research briefs / brief reports / brief communications (middle tier)**
- AJPM Research Briefs are "short reports of original empirical articles or evaluations": structured abstract ≤250 words, ≤1,200 words of text, limit of 4 tables/figures, "although most submissions do not exceed 2." AJPM also allows "Additional information, tables and figures ... as online-only appendix material" — [AJPM PDF r. 3/3/2023, fetched](https://legacyfileshare.elsevier.com/promis_misc/AMEPRE_gfa.pdf)
- PCD Research Brief (peer reviewed): unstructured abstract of 100 words ("no more than 4 sentences"); ≤1,200 words split into Objective 200 / Methods 300 / Results 300 / Discussion 400; ≤12 references; ≤3 tables/figures — [PCD Types of Articles, fetched](https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/for_authors/types_of_articles.htm)
- Annals of Epidemiology Brief Communication: 2,000 words, 200-word structured abstract, 3 tables/figures. It also lists "Rapid Reports" of 1,000 words — [Karim 2024, secondary](https://ehsanx.github.io/choosing-epidemiological-journals)
- Epidemiology Brief Reports: up to 1,500 words. It also has "Validation Studies" of 2,000 words — [Karim 2024, secondary](https://ehsanx.github.io/choosing-epidemiological-journals)
- Cultural Diversity & Ethnic Minority Psychology (APA): "psychometric investigations of culture-specific measures, or substantial cultural adaptation of existing measures are most suitable for brief reports" — [APA CDEMP submission page, search summary](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/cdp/submit). An unofficial mirror gives Brief Reports as 2,000–3,000 words including abstract, excluding references, tables and figures (unverified on apa.org) — [unofficial mirror](https://gamestartup.netlify.app/host-https-www.apa.org/pubs/journals/cdp)

**Research notes (survey methodology)**
- JSSAM research notes "should generally not exceed 3,000 words of text," excluding figures, tables, references and appendices, with "a limited number of figures and tables." Full articles may run to 6,500 words — [JSSAM Instructions to Authors, fetched](https://academic.oup.com/jssam/pages/General_Instructions)
- Survey Methods: Insights from the Field caps Research Notes at 2,500 words of text — [surveyinsights.org, search summary](https://surveyinsights.org/?p=522)

**Trend: preprints are replacing short "rapid" formats**
- A 2025 JECH editorial ("Revamping JECH's article types," Katikireddi & Pearce, JECH 79(12):897) retired Short Reports. It notes they were meant for rapid release of initial results, a role preprint servers now fill. Papers can still be submitted at ~1,500 words if that is enough — [JECH editorial, search summary; page blocked](https://jech.bmj.com/content/79/12/897). Earlier JECH limits for Short Reports were ≤1,500 words, 200-word abstract, ≤20 references and ≤2 display items — [Karim 2024, secondary](https://ehsanx.github.io/choosing-epidemiological-journals)

**Can methods go in a supplement?**
- AJPM: yes, as online-only appendix material, which "will not be typeset" — [AJPM PDF, fetched](https://legacyfileshare.elsevier.com/promis_misc/AMEPRE_gfa.pdf)
- JAMA Netw Open letters: only brief, essential methods; no extra results — [JNO IFA, fetched](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/pages/instructions-for-authors)
- PLOS ONE: "no restrictions on word count, number of figures, or amount of supporting information" — [PLOS ONE submission guidelines, fetched](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/submission-guidelines)
- JSSAM: appendices are excluded from the word count — [JSSAM, fetched](https://academic.oup.com/jssam/pages/General_Instructions)

### Inferences
- **How PH short formats compare with math letters journals** (the comparison with math is the author's framing; I found no sourced data on math-letters norms):
  - All the PH short formats above are peer reviewed and indexed as original research.
  - Research letters, however, have **no abstract**, which reduces discoverability.
  - They are tuned to one clean descriptive or associational result (a prevalence, a trend, an odds ratio). They are not built for a methods-heavy claim.
- **A DIF claim needs more space than a letter.** The planned paper's claim (uniform DIF on 3 items, replicated in 2 surveys, under MIMIC with anchors, plus MGCFA, anchor purification, alignment, replicate-weight variance and nativity adjustment) is exactly what reviewers will want to inspect, and anchor sensitivity in particular.
  - A 600–800-word letter with 1–2 display items cannot show both surveys' item-level DIF estimates and the anchor sensitivity results.
  - At JAMA Netw Open the sensitivity results cannot go in the supplement either (methods only).
  - So a letter is a poor fit. A Research Brief (AJPM: 1,200 words + structured abstract + up to 4 display items + online appendix) is the closest PH analogue to a substantive math letter that still works for this paper.
- **The 3,000-word version with 3–4 figures and 2 tables (5–6 display items)** is already at or above most journals' display-item caps: AJPM 4, PCD 5, JNO Original Investigation 5. Plan to move one or two display items to an appendix.
- **Speed and priority** in PH now come mainly from posting a preprint (see section 3), not from choosing a letter format. The JECH editorial says this explicitly.

### Gaps
- No sourced comparison of citation rates or perceived prestige of research letters versus full articles.
- No official, current (2026) word limits for Epidemiology, Annals of Epidemiology or JECH. Those pages blocked automated access; figures above come from a March 2024 compilation and a search summary.

## 2. Candidate journals: formats, limits, costs (incl. NYU deals), speed, scope, preprint policy, precedents

### Takeaway
On cost, three facts dominate:
- NYU Libraries' deals make **PLOS ONE free** for NYU-affiliated authors.
- **BMC/SpringerOpen** journals (BMC Public Health, Health & Justice) get only **15% off**.
- **PCD, Survey Research Methods and subscription-model AJPM charge nothing.**

There is **no NYU deal** with AMA/JAMA, Elsevier, SAGE, APA, BMJ, LWW, APHA or OUP. JAMA Network Open is the costliest at $4,000.

On fit, the best-documented options are:
- AJPM (Research Article or Research Brief, with an online appendix);
- PCD (Original Research or Research Brief; free; long BRFSS/ACE track record, but reported staff cuts);
- PLOS ONE (free via NYU, no length limits);
- JSSAM (research note, for a survey-methods audience).

## 3. Preprints: medRxiv vs PsyArXiv vs SocArXiv; do journals accept them; would a preprint protect priority?

### Takeaway
medRxiv is the natural server for a BRFSS/CHIS epidemiology paper:
- screening takes 2–4 days and posts get a DOI;
- JAMA Netw Open and PLOS have integrations;
- JNO, AJPM, PLOS ONE and JSSAM explicitly accept preprinted work.

A preprint creates a permanent, DOI-stamped public record. That is the best practical priority protection available, though journals are not obliged to honor it. PsyArXiv (pre-moderated since August 2025, typically 24–72 hours) is a reasonable alternative for a psychometrics framing. SocArXiv is a weaker fit.

### Cited Findings
**medRxiv**
- Accepts original research, systematic reviews, data articles and methods/protocol articles. It declines "narrative reviews and hypotheses, case reports, editorial/commentary/opinion articles," and "reserves the right to decline to post any preprint for any reason" — [medRxiv submit, fetched](https://www.medrxiv.org/submit-a-manuscript)
- Required declarations: ethical guidelines followed, necessary IRB approvals obtained, consent, competing interests, funding, and a data availability statement — [medRxiv submit, fetched](https://www.medrxiv.org/submit-a-manuscript)
- Ethics specifics — [medRxiv FAQ, fetched](https://www.medrxiv.org/about/FAQ):
  - A statement of ethical oversight is required "unless the data are already openly available."
  - Decisions from online tools are not accepted.
  - Anonymized data, surveys and **controlled-access datasets** are listed as needing oversight.
- Process — [medRxiv FAQ, fetched](https://www.medrxiv.org/about/FAQ):
  - "Article screening typically completes within 2-4 days."
  - Preprints "cannot be removed" (withdrawal notices only) and are cited by DOI.
  - "Many journals will consider manuscripts that were previously posted as preprints."
  - A paper already published in a journal cannot be posted.
- Direct transfer to participating journals is available (M2J) — [medRxiv submit, fetched](https://www.medrxiv.org/submit-a-manuscript)

**PsyArXiv**
- Moved to pre-moderation ("preprints are not available until after moderation") on 20 Aug 2025 because of "an influx of low-quality submissions." Moderation "is supposed to take about 24-72 hours" — [SIPS/PsyArXiv announcement, fetched](https://improvingpsych.org/2025/08/20/changes-to-moderation-at-psyarxiv-preprints/)
- Returned to normal operations in mid-September 2025. The most common rejection cause is a mismatch between the manuscript and the entered metadata. The policy was reportedly revised again in March 2026 (contents not confirmed) — [PsyArXiv returns to normal, search summary](https://improvingpsych.org/2025/09/17/psyarxiv-returns-to-normal-operations/)

**SocArXiv**
- Papers are moderated before posting; moderation is not peer review. The checklist covers: scholarly, in supported areas, plausibly categorized, correctly attributed, moderated language, text-searchable — [SocArXiv moderation policy, search summary](https://socopen.org/moderation-policy/)
- An ORCID requirement is planned, and AI-topic submissions were paused in November 2025 — [SocArXiv, search summary](https://socopen.org/moderation-policy/)

**Journal policies on preprints**
- JNO accepts preprinted manuscripts (link required) and offers medRxiv simultaneous submission — [JNO IFA, fetched](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/pages/instructions-for-authors)
- AJPM: preprints "can be shared anywhere at any time" and do not count as prior publication — [AJPM PDF, fetched](https://legacyfileshare.elsevier.com/promis_misc/AMEPRE_gfa.pdf)
- PLOS: "Posting does not impact consideration" — [PLOS ONE guidelines, fetched](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/submission-guidelines)
- JSSAM: authors "retain the right to make an Author's Original Version (preprint) available" — [JSSAM, fetched](https://academic.oup.com/jssam/pages/General_Instructions)
- PCD's editorial policy does not mention preprints — [PCD Editorial Policy, fetched](https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/about_the_journal/editorial_policy.htm)
- JECH's editors describe preprints as the replacement for rapid-release short reports — [JECH editorial, search summary](https://jech.bmj.com/content/79/12/897)

**Priority**
- ASAPbio: preprints are "permanent public documents with DOI." In physics, "arXiv postings are accepted as date-stamped priority claims." "Some journals offer 'scoop protection' policies that honor the priority claims of preprints." Scooping "is less prevalent than one might think" — [ASAPbio Preprint FAQ, fetched](https://asapbio.org/preprint-info/preprint-faq)

### Inferences
- **Posting timing:** given the Umoga (2026) dissertation embargo to April 2027, post to medRxiv at or before journal submission. It is the fastest credible way (days) to establish a dated, DOI-bearing public claim.
- **What priority does and does not mean:** priority credit in health sciences is a community norm, not a guarantee. A preprint does not stop a journal from publishing related work. It does make independent, earlier discovery documentable.
- **medRxiv ethics wording:** BRFSS files are fully open, so medRxiv's "openly available" carve-out likely applies. CHIS public-use files require a click-through acknowledgment (see section 5), so a short ethics statement citing an NYU "not human subjects research" determination avoids any screening question.
- **PsyArXiv vs medRxiv:** PsyArXiv is acceptable if framed as psychometrics, but medRxiv better matches PH/epi journals and has JNO/PLOS integrations. SocArXiv adds little here.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed: preprint policies for Epidemiology, Annals of Epidemiology, AJPH, SAGE, APA and Springer titles; the exact medRxiv form wording for public-data studies; PsyArXiv's March 2026 policy text.

## 4. OSF preregistration of secondary data analyses, and how prior data exposure is treated

### Takeaway
The standard tool is the OSF "Secondary Data Preregistration" template, based on Van den Akker et al. (2021, Meta-Psychology; 25 questions). It requires each author to disclose dates of data access, prior work on the dataset and prior knowledge.

Its guidance is blunt: when authors already have direct knowledge of the hypothesized association, they "should disregard doing a confirmatory analysis altogether or do one based on a different dataset."

The authors' frozen H1 plan says H1 was suggested by exploratory analyses of the same BRFSS/CHIS data on 2026-11-01. So the registration should be presented as a transparency and specification lock, not as a confirmatory test. None of the candidate PH journals requires preregistration for observational studies.

### Cited Findings
- OSF lists a "Secondary Data Preregistration" template "For preregistering a research project that uses an existing dataset." Registrations can be embargoed "for up to four years" — [OSF Help: registration templates, fetched](https://help.osf.io/article/229-select-a-registration-template)
- Van den Akker, Weston, Campbell, Chopik, Damian, Davis-Kean, Hall, Kosie, Kruse, Olsen, Ritchie, Valentine, van 't Veer & Bakker (2021), "Preregistration of secondary data analysis: A template and tutorial," *Meta-Psychology* 5, [doi:10.15626/MP.2020.2625](https://doi.org/10.15626/MP.2020.2625). The abstract notes that "researchers' hypotheses and analyses may be biased by their prior knowledge of the data" — [Meta-Psychology article page, fetched](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2625). Supplementary files are at [OSF U68Y7](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/U68Y7) — [article PDF, fetched](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2625/2508)
- Template items on data exposure — [article PDF, fetched](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2625/2508):
  - **Q6:** extent to which the data are publicly available, including barriers such as registration.
  - **Q8:** "date of download and/or access for each author."
  - **Q17:** prior publications, working papers, preprints and presentations using the dataset, per author.
  - **Q18:** "What prior knowledge do you have about the dataset that may be relevant for the proposed analysis?", answered per author.
  - **Q16:** sampling weights.
  - The template ends with a formal statement that it was filled out truthfully.
- Key guidance from the same paper — [article PDF, fetched](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2625/2508):
  - "where you have direct knowledge about a hypothesized association, you should disregard doing a confirmatory analysis altogether or do one based on a different dataset."
  - "Any indirect knowledge about the hypothesized association does not preclude a confirmatory analysis but should be transparently reported."
  - It defines undisclosed hypothesis generation from known data as HARKing, which "can lead to false positive results."
- The authors' plan (local file `/home/mo2273/ace_manuscript/prereg/H1_analysis_plan.md`, read directly):
  - Frozen 2026-11-02; disclosure: "On 2026-11-01 the authors ran exploratory analyses of the same data," and "Those analyses suggested H1."
  - None of the latent-variable models (MIMIC, MGCFA, replicate-weight variance) had been fitted when it was frozen.
- Registration is required only for trials in these journals: AJPM requires registration for clinical trials ([AJPM PDF](https://legacyfileshare.elsevier.com/promis_misc/AMEPRE_gfa.pdf)); PCD requires ClinicalTrials.gov registration for RCTs ([PCD Types](https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/for_authors/types_of_articles.htm)).

### Inferences
- **Framing in the manuscript:** by Van den Akker et al.'s own standard, the planned MIMIC test of H1 on the same BRFSS/CHIS data is not a fresh confirmatory test. The item-level DIF pattern was already seen in the logistic-DIF and 2PL exploratory runs.
  - Honest framing: "analysis plan registered (time-stamped, hashed) after exploratory screening; locks the estimator, anchors, decision rule and sensitivity analyses."
  - Avoid calling the results "confirmatory."
  - Reviewers in PH/epi rarely require preregistration, so an accurately labelled registration is a credibility plus. An over-claimed one is a liability.
- **A genuinely confirmatory component** would need data not yet examined, for example a future BRFSS or CHIS release or another survey fielding the ACE items in Spanish.
- **Embargo and priority:** the OSF embargo (up to 4 years) lets the authors timestamp the registration now without disclosing the finding. They can then make it public when the preprint posts.

### Gaps
- No candidate-journal policy found on how editors weigh preregistrations of secondary analyses with prior exposure.
- No empirical source found on reviewer reactions to such registrations in PH/epidemiology.

## 5. Ethics: does NYU treat public de-identified BRFSS/CHIS analysis as not human-subjects research, and what statement do journals expect?

### Takeaway
Yes, by NYU's own decision tree. For data "obtained from a producer or supplier of public use data" or "all ... available in the public domain," the outcome is "Project is Not Human Subjects Research. No application to the IRB office needed." The footnote says to contact NYU's Office of Sponsored Programs if a Data Use Agreement is required.

Journals still want an explicit statement. JAMA Netw Open says authors "should not make independent determinations of exemption," so the cleanest path is a short written determination from NYU HRPP, cited in Methods.

### Cited Findings

## 6. Final recommendation: top pick + 2 backups, and the plain answer on "letters/notes"

### Takeaway
**Plain answer:** letters exist in public health and epidemiology:
- **Research letters:** 600–800 words, 1–2 display items, no abstract, peer reviewed.
- **Research briefs / brief reports:** 1,200–2,000 words, with an abstract.
- **Research notes in survey methodology:** up to 3,000 words.

But the PH "research letter" is not a good home for this DIF paper. It cannot carry the item-level estimates for two surveys plus the anchor sensitivity analyses, and at JAMA Netw Open the supplement cannot hold results. Use a full article (~3,000 words), or at most a research brief with an online appendix. Get speed and priority from a medRxiv preprint, not from the letter format.

**Ranked picks:**
1. **AJPM Research Article,** or a Research Brief if they want it short. Free, credible, appendix-friendly, preprint-friendly.
2. **Backup 1: PCD Original Research,** or a Research Brief. Free, public domain, home of BRFSS/ACE work, ≤5 display items. Watch the reported staff cuts.
3. **Backup 2: PLOS ONE.** Free for NYU authors through 2027, no length or figure limits, roughly 6–7 weeks to first decision on older data.

If they want methodological prestige over PH reach, the "notes" option is a **JSSAM research note** (3,000 words; 3–4 months to decision).

### Cited Findings
- AJPM:
  - Research Article (≤3,000 words, ≤4 display items, structured abstract ≤250).
  - Research Brief (≤1,200 words, ≤4 display items) with online-only appendix allowed.
  - No fees for regular articles; preprints allowed anywhere, any time; external peer review.
  - Document dated 2023.

### Inferences
- **Why AJPM is the top pick for a first-time author from math:**
  - It is a recognizable, mainstream prevention/public-health journal, with no APC.
  - Its formats give a structured abstract plus an unlimited online appendix. The MIMIC/MGCFA specification, replicate-weight details and S1–S7 sensitivity analyses can live there while the main text stays at ~3,000 words with 4 display items.
  - It explicitly tolerates preprints, so the authors can post to medRxiv immediately.
  - The Research Brief (1,200 words + 4 display items + appendix) is the closest PH analogue to a substantive math "letter" if they want brevity.
  - Caveats: guidelines are from 2023, and speed and acceptance rate are unknown, so check the live site. If AJPM declines, AJPM Focus (OA, ~US$1,800) is the same-family fallback, but its format and transfer route are unverified.
- **Why PCD is backup 1:**
  - It is the CDC journal where BRFSS ACE analyses routinely appear, and it costs nothing.
  - Its 5-display-item cap accommodates 3 figures + 2 tables, and it has a Research Brief option.
  - Risk: reported editorial-staff terminations may slow review. Ask the editor about timelines and preprint policy before submitting.
- **Why PLOS ONE is backup 2:** zero cost through the NYU deal (renewal 12/31/2027), no space constraints for a methods-heavy paper, and integrated medRxiv workflows. The trade-off is lower field prestige than AJPM or PCD.
- **When to choose otherwise:**
  - For a measurement or survey-methods identity: JSSAM (research note) or Survey Research Methods (free; reviewers get 4 weeks).
  - For a cultural-measurement framing: a CDEMP Brief Report.
  - JAMA Netw Open Original Investigation offers top visibility and fast desk decisions, but is a long shot at 12% acceptance and costs $4,000.
- **Sequencing:**
  1. Get the NYU "not human subjects research" determination.
  2. Register the OSF secondary-data template under embargo, framed honestly given the 2026-11-01 exploratory runs.
  3. Post to medRxiv on submission day.
  4. Submit to AJPM.

- Not examined here: the Umoga (2026) dissertation's content and the overall novelty landscape.
