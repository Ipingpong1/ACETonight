# Deviations from, and clarifications of, the frozen H1 analysis plan

Plan: `/home/mo2273/ace_manuscript/prereg/H1_analysis_plan.md`. Its SHA-256 (`031cd720...d3b420fa`) was verified before the analysis started and the plan was not edited. No model, covariate, anchor, coding rule or decision rule was changed after seeing results.

## A. Deviations (implementation differs from the literal plan)

1. **BRFSS bootstrap: collapsed singleton strata.** The plan specifies 100 Rao–Wu rescaled bootstrap replicates with strata = year × file × _STSTR, PSU = _PSU and lonely PSUs adjusted. The analytic subsample has 2,837 such strata, and 749 of them contain a single PSU. `survey::as.svrepdesign(type = "subbootstrap")` resamples n_h − 1 PSUs per stratum and rescales by n_h/(n_h − 1), which is undefined for n_h = 1 (it yields NaN weights). The "adjust" lonely-PSU option exists only for Taylor linearization. **For the bootstrap only**, each singleton stratum was therefore merged with the nearest (by _STSTR code) multi-PSU stratum in the same year × file × state. If none existed there, it was merged with the nearest multi-PSU stratum in the same year × file. This left 2,088 bootstrap strata, each with at least 2 PSUs. Taylor-linearization analyses (Table 2) use the planned strata with `lonely.psu = "adjust"`. Check: the bootstrap SE of the weighted Spanish share was 0.0054 vs 0.0058 by Taylor, and of mental-illness prevalence 0.0044 vs 0.0041.
2. **BRFSS bootstrap: integer IDs passed to `as.svrepdesign`.** `survey:::subbootweights` is O(number of PSU factor levels) per stratum × replicate when the PSU ID is a factor (about 35k levels here). That would take hours for 100 replicates. Integer PSU and stratum IDs were passed instead, with PSUs unique within stratum (the same semantics as `nest = TRUE`). The algorithm and the function call are unchanged.

## B. Clarifications of details the plan leaves open (decided before the confirmatory models were run)

1. **Factor structure.** Ford et al. (2014) was checked in the full text (PMC4587306). The witnessed household violence item loads on Emotional/Physical Abuse (0.57; 0.33 on Household Dysfunction), and mental illness, alcohol, drugs, incarceration and separation/divorce load on Household Dysfunction. This matches the plan, so **no deviation** was needed.
2. **lavaan capability.** lavaan 0.7.2 supports `sampling.weights` with ordered indicators under WLSMV (checked: weighted and unweighted estimates differ). The planned estimator was used, so the fallback (mirt with survey weights) was **not** needed.
3. **Weights.** BRFSS final weight of the respondent's file ÷ 7; CHIS RAKEDW0–80 ÷ 5. The exploratory BRFSS analyses instead normalised weights to mean 1 within year. Weighted BRFSS percentages therefore differ slightly from the exploratory ones (e.g., household mental illness, Spanish interview: 4.0% here vs 4.7% exploratory). CHIS percentages reproduce the exploratory values exactly.
4. **Covariate coding.** Covariates are categorical dummies in native survey categories:
   - BRFSS: _AGEG5YR (13 groups), EDUCA (6), sex, income (5 groups plus missing as a category, per the plan), survey year (7), and Census region (4) derived from state FIPS.
   - CHIS: SRAGE_P1 (14 groups), AHEDC_P1 (9), SRSEX, POVLL (4), survey year (5).
   - The plan names "missing as a category" only for income. BRFSS age DK/refused (_AGEG5YR = 14, n = 234) and education refused (EDUCA = 9, n = 177) were likewise kept as their own categories, which retains the full planned analytic sample (n = 10,945 / 24,473).
5. **Identification and scale.** Factor residual variances are fixed at 1 (`std.lv = TRUE`), so all loadings are free. The theta parameterization fixes the latent-response residual variances at 1, so the direct effect b is a probit coefficient at equal factor score and covariates.
6. **p-values.** Two-sided Wald z tests using the replicate SE; Holm adjustment across the 6 target tests (3 items × 2 surveys). Replicate variance uses MSE = TRUE (deviations from the full-sample estimate) in both surveys. The plan states this for CHIS; for BRFSS it is the conservative choice.
7. **"Partially supported".** The rule "these criteria hold for at least 2 items in both surveys" was read as the same ≥ 2 items meeting all criteria in both surveys. The per-survey count reading is reported too, along with whether the verdict depends on the reading.
8. **Model-implied probability difference.** For each Spanish-interview respondent, P(endorse | covariates) was computed with and without the direct effect, marginalising over the factor residual: Φ((λΓ'x + b − τ)/√(λ²ψ + 1)) vs the same with b = 0. These were then averaged with the survey weights; replicate SEs come from the same replicate coefficient sets.
9. **S3 MGCFA (binary items, theta parameterization).** Latent-response residual variances are fixed at 1 in both groups because they are not identified for binary items. In the scalar models, the Spanish group's latent means and variances are free (English: 0/1). The partial scalar model frees the loading and threshold of the three target items.
10. **S4b purification.** At each iteration, every remaining anchor was tested by freeing its direct effect one at a time. Among those with p < .01 and |b| ≥ 0.24, the single item with the largest |b| was freed. This repeated until no item met the criterion. At least one anchor was always kept per factor (identification). Iteration 1 is identical to S4c.
11. **S4d alignment.** `sirt::invariance.alignment` was run per factor on the configural two-group (English/Spanish) WLSMV theta estimates, with no covariates and intercept ν = −threshold. With only two groups, alignment is weakly informative; it is reported as a descriptive anchor-free check.
12. **S6 mode.** To estimate a Spanish × web interaction on the target direct effects, the model also includes the web main effect and the Spanish × web product: (a) as covariates of both factors and (b) as direct effects on the three target items. The Spanish direct effect is then reported for phone (b_sp) and web (b_sp + b_sp×web) interviews.
13. **S5 impact.** Uses the 8 harmonized items (count 0–8 and ≥ 4 of those 8), as instructed. It has 50 simulation draws per respondent with common random numbers across scenarios and replicates, and CIs from the same replicate coefficient sets as the primary analysis.
14. **Table 2.** Prevalence CIs use the logit method (`svyciprop`), and PR CIs are Wald. A CHIS column adjusting additionally for nativity and % life in the US was **added as context and is labelled as not pre-specified**.
15. **Secondary SEs.** As the plan allows, S3, S4, S6 and S7 use lavaan weight-robust SEs. S1 and S2 use CHIS jackknife replicate SEs, with the lavaan SE also reported.
16. **S3 implementation (lavaan behaviour, not a change of specification).** With these binary indicators, lavaan 0.7.2's `group.equal = c("loadings", "thresholds")` constrained only the loadings. The scalar model had df = 44 instead of 50, and the Spanish latent means stayed fixed at 0. The three MGCFA models were therefore written with explicit cross-group equality labels and checked by their degrees of freedom (configural 38, full scalar 50, partial scalar 44, as expected). The first, incorrect S3 run is marked "SUPERSEDED" in STATUS.md, and only the corrected run is reported.
17. **S4 run in parts.** S4 was run as three parallel jobs (BRFSS, CHIS, CHIS + nativity) and then merged. The models and the procedure are unchanged.

## C. Interpretive note (not a deviation)

The plan states that if the anchors are under-reported in Spanish, the target estimates are biased toward zero (conservative). S4c shows a different pattern in BRFSS: the anchor *problem drinking* is over-endorsed in Spanish relative to the factor (b = +0.61), and separation/divorce is under-endorsed (b = −0.26). The primary BRFSS target estimates are therefore not guaranteed to be conservative. Under the pre-specified purified-anchor model (S4b), they shrink to b ≈ −0.14 to −0.22 and are not significant. This is reported in RESULTS.md alongside the primary verdict. The decision rule itself was applied exactly as frozen.
